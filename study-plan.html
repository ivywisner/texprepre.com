Before Any Plan

Start With a Baseline, Not a Guess

Every plan below starts the same way: find out what you'd score today. Not on your favorite topic — on a mix. That replaces "I feel pretty ready?" with an actual number, which is the difference between a study plan and a vibe. The fastest way to get one is our free 16-question Texas diagnostic — no email, with an instant topic breakdown showing exactly where your points are leaking.

One more thing before the timelines: the Texas exam is really two exams in one sitting — an 85-question national section and a 40-question Texas section, scored separately, with 70% required on each. A great national score can't rescue a weak Texas-law score, so practice both from day one and give the state section — contracts, license law, agency — the respect it demands. (There's real math on this exam, too. Not a lot, but enough to punish anyone who skips it.)

30+ Days Out

The 30-Day Texas Real Estate Exam Study Plan

Best fit: your course is done (or nearly), your exam is a month or more away, and you want to build a passing score — not just protect one.

Week 1 — Diagnose. Take your baseline, then drill your two weakest topics with focused quizzes. Ten questions at a time beats hour-long marathons: you'll actually remember why each answer was wrong.

Week 2 — Repair. Keep drilling weak topics and start flashcards for vocabulary — the exam leans hard on terminology, and the same 50 terms keep showing up. Reviewing every question you miss matters more than the number of questions you take.

Week 3 — Mix. Switch from topic drills to full-length practice exams, where the topics arrive shuffled like the real thing. The exam never tells you which chapter a question came from; from here on, neither should your practice.

Week 4 — Prove it. Early in the week, sit a full-length timed practice exam — all 125 questions, the whole 4-hour clock, one sitting — phone timers and snack breaks are exam-day lies. Patch what it exposes, then keep the practice exams coming right through the final day — exam eve is a study day, not a rest day. Close the books at a reasonable hour and get real sleep.

Three Weeks Out

The 21-Day Plan: Repair, Mix, Rehearse

Best fit: coursework finished, exam booked, scoring somewhere respectable-but-not-safe on fresh questions.

Week 1 — Find and fix the leaks. Baseline first, then give each of your two or three weakest topics its own focused days. Read every explanation on every miss — that's where the actual studying happens.

Week 2 — Go mixed. Full-length practice exams every couple of days, with your missed-question pile reviewed between them. Your score on fresh mixed questions is the only number that predicts exam day — and check it on both sections, not just the overall.

Week 3 — Under the clock. One full-length 125-question timed practice exam early in the week, another near the end, light review between. Keep drilling straight through exam eve — practice exams are the best use of the last day, not the thing to quit early. Handle logistics along the way, then call it at a reasonable bedtime.

Two Weeks Out

The 14-Day Plan: Compress What You Know

Best fit: the material isn't new — you need a disciplined review, not a first pass. Two weeks compresses; it doesn't teach.

Days 1–5: Baseline, then hit your weakest two topics hard while keeping one short mixed session in the rotation daily so nothing else rusts.

Days 6–10: Mostly mixed practice exams now, with missed-question review after each. Flashcards fill the small gaps in your day — the line at the pharmacy is a vocab session.

Days 11–14: One full timed 125 around day 11 or 12. Patch the two or three topics it flags, then fill the remaining days with practice exams and missed-question review — including the day before the exam. The only cutoff that matters is a reasonable bedtime.

One Week Left

The 7-Day Plan: Triage, Don't Panic

Best fit: you're near the passing range and the week's job is repair and rehearsal — not learning real estate from scratch.

Days 1–2: A fresh mixed session to spot your two biggest leaks, then drill only those. A week rewards ruthless triage: fixing your worst topic is worth more points than polishing your best five.

Days 3–5: One full-length timed practice exam, done exactly like test day — one sitting, four hours, no pausing. Then review every miss until you can explain why the right answer is right.

Days 6–7: Practice exams and missed-question review straight through the day before the exam — if you can give exam eve the whole day, give it the whole day. Confirm your appointment, your route, and what to bring along the way, then get to bed at a reasonable hour.

48 Hours Before the Exam

The Final 48 Hours

Best fit: the material is in there — these two days are for reps and repair, right up to the end.

The first day: Practice exams, with every miss reviewed until you can explain why the right answer is right. Your missed-question pile and flashcards fill the gaps between runs — the highest-value minutes available this late.

Exam eve: Study the whole day if you can, ideally with more practice exams. Confirm the appointment time, IDs, calculator, and route (the exam-day checklist covers everything), don't skip meals, and go to bed at a reasonable hour. Tomorrow, 125 questions and a 4-hour clock — and you'll have more in the tank than you think.

Real Life Doesn't Pause

Studying With a Job, a Toddler, or a Brain That Wanders

Every plan above assumes tidy, uninterrupted study blocks. Most real lives don't have those — and the fix is the same in every case: shrink the unit of studying until it fits the life you actually have.

Working full-time? Twenty focused minutes on weeknights — one 10-question quiz plus reviewing your misses — moves the needle more than a guilt-fueled Saturday marathon. Save one weekend morning for the full-length practice runs; protect it like a shift.

Studying with a baby or toddler at home? Nap windows are quiz windows. Short sessions exist precisely because study time arrives in unpredictable 15-minute pieces — and progress that saves itself means an interruption costs you nothing but the interruption.

ADHD, or focus that fights you? Shorten the loop, not the standard: quick quiz, instant feedback, streak intact, done. Ten engaged minutes beat an hour of rereading the same paragraph — and a visible streak does more for tomorrow's session than willpower ever has.

Pregnant, exhausted, or just running on fumes? Study your energy windows, whenever they land, and let the plan flex around the week you're actually having. Consistency-ish wins: a five-question day still keeps the material warm, and warm is what you need on exam day.

🔁

If the exam goes wrong, the plan doesn't restart — it shortens. Over half of first-timers don't pass the Texas exam, and you only retake the section you failed.
