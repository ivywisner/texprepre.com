Short Answer
Plan on $300–$1,000 for the six required courses, and a couple of months to get through them. Every TREC-approved school teaches the same required 180 hours — price buys polish and support, not different material. And the timeline has a legal floor: TREC caps coursework at 12 hours a day, so nobody legally finishes the education in under 15 days — and almost nobody actually goes that fast.
The Price

Same Curriculum, Very Different Price Tags

Texas requires six 30-hour courses — Principles of Real Estate I and II, Law of Agency, Law of Contracts, Promulgated Contract Forms, and Real Estate Finance — from a TREC-approved provider. That's the whole requirement, and it's identical everywhere: a $300 package and a $1,000 package teach to the same state-mandated curriculum. What the extra money buys is production quality, support, exam-prep add-ons, and sometimes a live classroom — worth paying for if they help you actually finish, but never different material.

Most online students land in the lower half of the range, and the six courses almost always cost less bought as a package than one at a time. Classroom programs — which still have a real following in Texas — sit at the top of the range and run on the school's schedule instead of yours.

The Real Timeline

The Clock Nobody Advertises

Here's what the marketing pages leave out. TREC caps course time at 12 hours per day, which puts a hard 15-day legal floor under the 180 hours — and that floor assumes someone doing nothing but real estate school, twelve hours a day, for two straight weeks. Real life doesn't run at that pace.

Six separate courses also means six separate course finals, each with its own scheduling and completion paperwork before the next certificate lands. For most people studying around a job, the education realistically takes a couple of months — it's the single biggest block of time in the whole licensing timeline.

💡

The Texas timeline trick: overlap the paperwork. Your TREC application ($206) and fingerprints don't have to wait for your certificates — file while you're still mid-course and the state's processing time runs in parallel instead of in series. The full timeline guide shows how the overlap saves weeks.

Online vs. Classroom

It's (Mostly) All Online Now

Most Texans now do the entire 180 hours online — the courses, the course finals, and the certificates, all from home. Texas is one of the few states where big classroom schools still thrive, and some people genuinely learn better in a room — but you pay for the room, and you drive to it. The only step that must happen in person either way is the state exam itself, at a Pearson VUE test center. If you were searching for a fully online Texas real estate license: the education half is, the exam half isn't.
